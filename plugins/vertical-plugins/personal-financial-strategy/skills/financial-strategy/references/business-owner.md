# Business-Owner Module (W-2 job + LLC self-employment)

Last verified: 2026-07-28. TY2026 limits from IRS Notice 2025-67.

## The core mechanic — get this right first
- The **§402(g) elective-deferral limit** — $24,500 (+ catch-up) — is **per person across ALL 401(k)/403(b) plans**.
- The **§415(c) annual-additions limit** — $72,000 — applies **separately per unrelated employer**.
- Consequence: someone can max deferrals at the W-2 employer's plan AND still receive employer (profit-sharing) contributions to a Solo 401(k) on LLC self-employment income, up to a separate $72,000 ceiling on that side.
- **Cross-foot every contribution plan before delivery:** W-2 deferrals + Solo 401(k) deferrals ≤ $24,500 (+ catch-up); each employer's total additions ≤ $72,000; employer contribution ≤ the deduction limit on net self-employment income. State each leg of the check.
- **Controlled/affiliated-service-group check (IRC §414(b), (c), (m)):** the separate per-employer §415(c) limit holds only if the W-2 employer and the LLC are genuinely unrelated. Common ownership or an affiliated-service arrangement collapses the two into one limit. Flag this as a confirm-with-the-CPA item on any dual-plan strategy — it is the load-bearing assumption (see `references/authorities.md`).

## Employer-contribution math (sole proprietor / single-member LLC)
The deductible employer contribution is ≈ **20% of net self-employment earnings** — the statutory 25%-of-compensation limit applied after reducing income by ½ of SE tax and by the contribution itself. Show the computation; never hand-wave the 25%-vs-20% distinction, and never apply 25% directly to Schedule C net profit.

## Plan selection

| Plan | Best when | Key TY2026 limits | Cautions |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Solo 401(k)** | Owner (+ spouse) only; wants maximum space, Roth options, or mega-backdoor | Employee deferral $24,500 (shared across employers) + employer contribution, to $72,000 combined per employer | Plan document must allow after-tax + in-plan Roth for mega-backdoor; Form 5500-EZ filing once plan assets exceed $250,000 |
| **SEP-IRA** | Simplicity; no deferral component needed | Employer-only, ≤25% of comp (≈20% of net SE), max $72,000 | Less total space at moderate income (no deferral leg); a SEP balance poisons backdoor-Roth pro-rata math |
| **SIMPLE IRA** | A small team of employees | $17,000 deferral (+$4,000 catch-up; $5,250 at 60–63) | Lower ceilings; 2-year rollover restriction on new accounts |
| **DB / cash balance** | High, stable SE income seeking large deductions | Funds toward a $290,000 annual benefit (§415(b)) | Actuarial cost and a multi-year funding commitment; layer over a DC plan only with an actuary |

Default comparison for this household shape (W-2 deferrals already used): Solo 401(k) generally dominates SEP-IRA — same employer-contribution ceiling, plus the Roth/mega-backdoor optionality, minus the pro-rata contamination.

## S-corp election tradeoffs
Payroll-tax savings on distributions must be weighed against four coupled effects: (1) the reasonable-compensation requirement (owner W-2 wages, defensible against IRS scrutiny); (2) retirement contributions keyed to those W-2 wages — lower wages shrink the plan base; (3) QBI computed after owner wages; (4) payroll and administrative cost. Model all four together, then route the election decision to the CPA (execution = hard route).

## QBI (§199A) for a consulting LLC
Consulting is a **specified service trade or business (SSTB)**. The 20% deduction is permanent (OBBBA), with a $400 minimum deduction where active QBI ≥ $1,000. The SSTB benefit phases out across a $75,000 (single) / $150,000 (MFJ) range beginning at the indexed §199A taxable-income threshold — VERIFY the current-year threshold before computing a specific deduction. Note the coupling: retirement contributions and entity choices lower taxable income and can restore QBI inside the phase-out band — optimize jointly, not sequentially.

## Washington notes
- LLC self-employment income is exempt from WA Cares unless the owner opts in.
- The LLC is disregarded for the WA capital-gains excise tax — gains land on the owner's return (see `washington.md`).
- A business interest acquired during marriage is presumptively community property — it matters for both estate math and any future sale.

## Succession / exit basics
Buy-sell agreement (funded by life insurance or installments), periodic business valuation, and coordination of entity value with the estate plan — Washington's ~$3M estate exclusion means a business interest crosses the threshold quickly. Document drafting is a hard route to the attorney; valuation methodology and deal structure education are in scope here.
