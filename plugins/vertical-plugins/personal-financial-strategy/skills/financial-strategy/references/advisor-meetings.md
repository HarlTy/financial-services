# Advisor Relationships & Meeting Prep

Last verified: 2026-07-28.

## Standards of care
- **RIA** (registered under the Investment Advisers Act of 1940 — the federal statute governing investment advisers): fiduciary duty at all times.
- **Broker-dealer**: Regulation Best Interest ("Reg BI," 2020) — stronger than the old suitability standard, weaker than a full fiduciary duty.
- **Hybrid / dually registered** advisors switch standards by account type — the norm on large independent broker-dealer platforms. Always ask which standard applies to each specific account.
- A **CFP® professional** commits to fiduciary conduct whenever giving financial advice, regardless of firm type. The strongest structural alignment is a fee-only RIA who is also a CFP®.

## Fee benchmarks (Kitces Research, 2024)
Median blended AUM fee ≈ **1% on portfolios up to $1M**, declining toward ~0.5% at high-net-worth levels. Hourly median **$300**. Standalone plan median **$3,000** (~$2,750 simple to $3,500+ extensive). Subscription/retainer median **$4,500/year**. The **all-in cost** (advisory fee + underlying fund expense ratios + platform/transaction costs) typically runs well above the headline — Kitces/Veres estimate ≈1.65% average. Always convert to all-in dollars per year.

## Disclosures & verification
**Form ADV Part 2** (the plain-English brochure: services, fees, conflicts, disciplinary history) and **Form CRS** (whether the firm acts as adviser, broker, or both — and how it is paid). Verify the firm and the individual at SEC IAPD and FINRA BrokerCheck before engaging, and re-check annually.

## Credentials decoder
CFP® — comprehensive planning (education + board exam + ~6,000 hours experience + ethics; fiduciary commitment when advising) · CFA — investment-analysis depth · ChFC — planning coursework, no single board exam · CPA/PFS — CPAs only; tax-integrated planning · AIF — fiduciary-process training · NAPFA membership — fee-only plus a signed fiduciary oath.

## Question bank — initial / evaluation meeting
1. Are you a fiduciary 100% of the time, for every one of my accounts — and will you put that in writing?
2. Exactly how are you compensated (fee-only / fee-based / commission), and by whom besides me?
3. What is my ALL-IN annual cost in dollars — advisory fee plus fund expenses plus platform/trading?
4. Who is the custodian, and how are my assets titled and protected?
5. What is your written investment process and rebalancing discipline?
6. How will you coordinate with my CPA and estate attorney — and have you done that with other clients?
7. What planning assumptions (returns, inflation, longevity) drive my plan, and can I see them?
8. May I have your Form ADV Part 2 and Form CRS?
9. Who covers my account when you're unavailable, and what is your succession plan?
10. What kind of client do you NOT serve well?

## Question bank — annual review
1. Performance versus the agreed benchmark, net of ALL fees — and is that benchmark still the right one?
2. What did we pay in total last year, in dollars?
3. What tax moves did you execute or deliberately defer (harvesting, conversions, asset location) — and why?
4. Are we still on plan (Monte Carlo / funded status), and which assumption changed most since last year?
5. What would change if I retired three years earlier — or later?
6. What rebalancing actions were taken, and what drift tolerance are you running?
7. What in my situation do you need updated (income, entity, family, goals)?
8. Which upcoming law changes affect me? (Current-cycle examples: OBBBA temporary provisions and sunsets, the WA estate-rate rollback, ACA credit expiration, the 2026 Roth catch-up mandate.)

## Document checklist (bring / refresh)
Latest tax returns · pay stubs and W-2s · LLC Schedule C or K-1 · all investment and retirement account statements · Social Security statements (ssa.gov) · insurance policies (life, disability, umbrella, LTC) · estate documents (wills, trusts, POAs, beneficiary designations) · debt statements · employer benefits summary · budget / cash-flow summary.

## Red flags
Won't confirm fiduciary status in writing · vague fee answers or headline-fee-only quotes · product pitches (annuities, insurance) before a written plan exists · no repeatable documented process · disciplinary history on BrokerCheck · performance promises · pressure and artificial urgency · reluctance to coordinate with your CPA or attorney.

## Meeting-prep packet (what this skill generates)
One page, in this order:
1. **Agenda topics**, ranked by consequence.
2. **Decisions pending**, each with the inputs already gathered in this conversation.
3. **Tailored question list** drawn from the banks above (initial or annual as appropriate).
4. **Documents to bring**, filtered to the agenda.
5. **Changes since last meeting** — income, entity, family, goals, account moves.
6. **Figures verified this session**, each with its tax year and FINAL/VERIFY status.
Deliver as chat text by default; as a file on request.
