# Meeting-Prep Packet — First Meeting with a Wealth Manager

## 1. Agenda topics, ranked by consequence

1. **The engagement itself — fiduciary standard and all-in cost.** This gates everything else: an advisor who is a fiduciary only part of the time, or whose real cost is 1.65% rather than the 1% quoted, changes every downstream number. Decide this before you discuss portfolios.
2. **Washington estate exposure.** WA imposes an estate tax with a $3,000,000 exclusion and **no portability between spouses** — an exclusion unused at the first death is simply lost. Federal exemption is $15,000,000 per person (TY2026). For most affected WA households the state tax, not the federal, is the binding constraint, and a plan built around the federal number misses it entirely. 2026 is a split year: deaths 7/1/2025–6/30/2026 face a top rate of 35% above $9M; deaths on/after 7/1/2026 face graduated rates of 10%–20% under the ESB 6347 rollback (signed 3/24/2026). A closely held LLC interest crosses $3M quickly.
3. **Dual-plan retirement capacity (W-2 + LLC).** The §402(g) elective-deferral limit of $24,500 (TY2026) is shared across all your 401(k)-type plans; the §415(c) annual-additions limit of $72,000 (TY2026) applies **separately per unrelated employer**. So you can max deferrals at the W-2 plan and still take employer/profit-sharing contributions into a Solo 401(k) on LLC income against its own $72,000 ceiling. Two live sub-issues: the controlled-group question (below), and the **mandatory Roth catch-up effective 2026** — if prior-year W-2 Box 3 wages from that employer exceeded $150,000, catch-up contributions must be Roth (401(k)/403(b)/457(b) only; not SEP or SIMPLE; tested per employer).
4. **Asset location under the WA capital-gains excise.** WA levies 7% of "Washington capital gains" plus an additional 2.90% on the portion above $1,000,000 — and that $1M threshold is measured **after** the standard deduction, not on gross gain. Retirement accounts and real estate are exempt from the excise; taxable brokerage assets are not. That makes location decisions worth more in WA than the generic federal answer implies. Municipal bonds carry no state-tax advantage here — compare after-tax yields on the federal exemption alone.
5. **Community property and basis.** WA is a community property state, so both halves of community property receive a full basis step-up at the first death (IRC §1014(b)(6)). That is a significant income-tax advantage and a reason to be careful before retitling appreciated community assets as separate property. An LLC interest acquired during marriage is presumptively community property.
6. **Protection gaps.** Disability insurance protects your largest asset (remaining lifetime earnings) and matters more with self-employment income; own-occupation definitions preferred. WA Cares pays up to $36,500 lifetime (claimable from July 1, 2026) — a partial offset, not long-term-care coverage. LLC self-employment income is not subject to the 0.58% WA Cares payroll tax unless you opt in. Umbrella liability at least at net-worth scale.
7. **Education funding.** 529 mechanics and the 529→Roth rollover ($35,000 lifetime per beneficiary, capped annually by the Roth limit — $7,500 for TY2026 — 15-year account age, last-5-years contributions ineligible).
8. **Process and assumptions.** Which return, inflation, and longevity assumptions drive your plan; what rebalancing discipline and drift tolerance they run; how they coordinate with your CPA and attorney. Also note TY2026 changed charitable math: non-itemizers get $1,000/$2,000, and itemizers face a new 0.5%-of-AGI floor, so any prior bunching analysis needs re-running.

## 2. Decisions pending — intake prompts

No scenario or figures were worked in this conversation, so there is nothing here to report as gathered inputs. These are the inputs to assemble before you walk in — answer them for yourself first; the advisor's value comes from what you bring, not from what they guess:

- Ages for both spouses, and target retirement age for each.
- Current annual W-2 wages, and LLC net profit (Schedule C or K-1 line).
- Account balances sorted by **tax bucket**: taxable / tax-deferred / Roth / HSA. This grouping, not a single net-worth number, is what makes conversion and location analysis possible.
- Whether the LLC and the W-2 employer share any common ownership or an affiliated-service arrangement (see §7 — this is load-bearing).
- Whether a Solo 401(k), SEP, or SIMPLE already exists, and whether any pre-tax IRA/SEP/SIMPLE balance exists (it contaminates backdoor-Roth pro-rata math).
- Estimated combined estate value including the LLC interest and real estate — specifically, is it above or below $3,000,000?
- Whether wills, trusts, POAs, and beneficiary designations exist, when they were last reviewed, and whether a community property agreement is in place.
- Existing insurance: life (type and death benefit), disability (own-occupation or not), umbrella limit, any LTC policy.
- Annual essential spending, and months of it currently held in cash.
- Any planned large capital-gain realization or business sale, and its likely year.

## 3. Questions to ask — initial / evaluation meeting

Core ten:
1. Are you a fiduciary 100% of the time, for every one of my accounts — and will you put that in writing?
2. Exactly how are you compensated (fee-only / fee-based / commission), and by whom besides me?
3. What is my **all-in** annual cost in dollars — advisory fee plus fund expense ratios plus platform and trading costs?
4. Who is the custodian, and how are my assets titled and protected?
5. What is your written investment process and rebalancing discipline?
6. How will you coordinate with my CPA and estate attorney — and have you actually done that with other clients?
7. What planning assumptions (returns, inflation, longevity) drive my plan, and can I see them?
8. May I have your Form ADV Part 2 and Form CRS?
9. Who covers my account when you're unavailable, and what is your succession plan?
10. What kind of client do you *not* serve well?

Tailored to this household:
11. **Asset location:** retirement accounts and real estate sit outside the WA capital-gains excise; taxable accounts don't. Does that change where you'd hold appreciating assets relative to the generic federal answer?
12. **Dual-plan design:** how would you structure the Solo 401(k) side against the W-2 plan — and would you draft the plan document to permit after-tax contributions plus in-plan Roth conversion (the mega-backdoor route)?
13. **Long-term care:** WA Cares pays up to $36,500 lifetime against an exposure many multiples of that. What does the remaining gap look like, and does self-employment opt-in change it?
14. **Estate coordination:** WA gives no portability. Will you work with our attorney on preserving both exclusions, and do you model the state estate tax at all — or only the federal exemption?

## 4. Documents to bring

Filtered to the agenda above: latest tax returns · recent pay stubs and W-2s (Box 3 matters for the Roth catch-up test) · LLC Schedule C or K-1 · all investment and retirement account statements · Social Security statements from ssa.gov · insurance policies (life, disability, umbrella, LTC) · estate documents — wills, trusts, POAs, **beneficiary designations**, and any community property agreement · debt statements · employer benefits summary (does the 401(k) allow after-tax contributions and in-plan Roth conversion?) · a budget or cash-flow summary.

**Before the meeting, verify independently:** the firm and the individual at SEC IAPD (adviserinfo.sec.gov) and FINRA BrokerCheck. Re-check annually.

**Red flags to watch for in the room:** won't confirm fiduciary status in writing · vague fee answers or a headline-only fee quote · product pitches (annuities, insurance) before a written plan exists · no repeatable documented process · disciplinary history · performance promises · pressure and artificial urgency · reluctance to coordinate with your CPA or attorney.

## 7. Authorities in play

Citations from this skill's authority index — text not re-verified this session (no retrieval tool available). Pattern: name the authority, state the fact, hand the professional the judgment call.

- **For the CPA — dual-plan strategy.** IRC §414(b), (c), (m). "Confirm there's no controlled-group or affiliated-service-group relationship between my W-2 employer and my LLC — the separate $72,000 §415(c) limit for a Solo 401(k) depends on the two being unrelated." This is the single assumption the whole dual-plan strategy rests on.
- **For the estate attorney — WA portability gap.** RCW 83.100; IRC §2518 (qualified disclaimers); IRC §2056(b)(7) (QTIP). "Washington gives us no portability. Does our current plan actually preserve both exclusions — credit-shelter, disclaimer, or QTIP — and does the answer change depending on whether the first death falls before or after 7/1/2026?"
- **For the estate attorney — marital property.** RCW 26.16; RCW 26.16.120 (community property agreements); IRC §1014(b)(6) (double step-up). "Have we confirmed the character of assets acquired during the marriage, including the LLC interest, and would a community property agreement strengthen or conflict with the trust design we have?"
- **For the CPA — gain timing.** RCW 82.87 (rate mechanics at RCW 82.87.040 as amended by ESSB 5813 (2025) sec. 101; defined terms at RCW 82.87.020; allocation at RCW 82.87.060). "For a planned sale, what does the WA excise cost realized in one tax year versus split across two — accounting for both the annual standard deduction and the $1M surcharge tier measured post-deduction?"
- **For the CPA — Roth catch-up compliance.** IRC §402A; SECURE 2.0 §603. "For 2026, does my employer's plan have the Roth catch-up mechanics in place, and does my prior-year Box 3 wage figure put me over the $150,000 test?"
- **For the advisor — standard of care.** Investment Advisers Act of 1940, 15 U.S.C. §80b-1 et seq. (antifraud basis §80b-6); Regulation Best Interest, 17 C.F.R. §240.15l-1. "Which standard applies to each specific account you'd open for me — Advisers Act fiduciary or Reg BI? Put the answer in writing, account by account." Hybrid/dually-registered advisors switch standards by account type, and that is the norm on large independent broker-dealer platforms.
- **On the horizon, for both.** ESSB 6346 (2026) would impose a 9.9% WA tax on income over $1M effective 2028; a repeal initiative (I-645) is on the **November 3, 2026** ballot and a constitutional challenge is pending. Treat it as unsettled — a plan that assumes it either survives or dies is a plan making a bet. Note it is a proposed **income** tax, distinct from the capital-gains excise surcharge that happens to share the 9.9% figure. Also live: the enhanced ACA premium tax credits expired 12/31/2025 (relevant if either of you might retire before 65), and OBBBA's temporary provisions run through 2028 with the SALT cap reverting in 2030.

---

**One structural note on how to use the hour.** A first meeting is a mutual evaluation, and the strongest structural alignment available is a fee-only RIA who is also a CFP® professional — a CFP® commits to fiduciary conduct whenever giving financial advice regardless of firm type. Do not let the conversation move to specific funds or products before you have the fiduciary answer in writing, the all-in cost in dollars, and Form ADV Part 2 plus Form CRS in hand. If a product is pitched before a written plan exists, that ordering is itself the information.
