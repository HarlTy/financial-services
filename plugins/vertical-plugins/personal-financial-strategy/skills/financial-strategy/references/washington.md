# Washington State Module

Last verified: 2026-07-28. Sources: WA Dept. of Revenue; RCW 82.87 (capital gains excise); RCW 83.100 (estate tax); ESSB 5813 (2025); ESB 6347 (2026); SB 5291 (2025); ESSB 6346 (2026).

Washington has **no state income tax on wages** — but three WA-specific regimes routinely change the answer for a WA household, and two of them were amended within the last 13 months. Never give an estate, capital-gains, or LTC answer to a WA resident without this file.

## 1. Capital gains excise tax (RCW 82.87; ESSB 5813, retroactive to 1/1/2025)
- **Rates:** 7% on Washington-allocated long-term capital gains above the standard deduction; **9.9%** on the portion above $1,000,000 (the 2.9-point surcharge added in 2025). The $1M surcharge threshold is NOT indexed.
- **Standard deduction:** $278,000 for TY2025, CPI-indexed — VERIFY the TY2026 amount at DOR before computing.
- **Exempt:** real estate; assets held in retirement accounts; livestock/agricultural assets; timber; commercial fishing privileges; plus deduction treatment tied to qualified family-owned small business sales — VERIFY the exact category fit against RCW 82.87 before relying on an exemption.
- **Who pays:** individuals only, on long-term gains. A single-member LLC is disregarded — its gains land on the owner's return.
- **Filing:** returns due April 15 following the tax year (first filings were April 15, 2026 for TY2025).
- **Planning notes:** timing gains across years manages both the annual standard deduction and the $1M surcharge tier; the retirement-account and real-estate exemptions make asset-location choices materially more valuable in WA than in most states.

## 2. Estate tax (RCW 83.100) — split-year 2026
- **Deaths 7/1/2025 – 6/30/2026:** exclusion $3,000,000, CPI-indexed (≈$3,076,000 for early-2026 deaths — VERIFY the DOR table); top rate **35%** on the portion above $9M (ESSB 5813 — briefly the highest state rate in the nation).
- **Deaths on/after 7/1/2026:** exclusion $3,000,000 (Seattle-area CPI indexed); graduated rates **10%–20%**, top rate 20% (ESB 6347 rollback, signed 3/24/2026).
- **NO portability between spouses.** An exemption unused at the first death is lost. Standard responses: credit-shelter/bypass trust, disclaimer trust, and QTIP election planning to preserve both spouses' exclusions — drafting is a hard route to the estate attorney.
- **The federal/WA gap:** federal exemption $15M vs WA ~$3M means the WA tax is the binding constraint for most affected households; a plan built only around the federal number misses the state exposure entirely. A closely held business interest can push an estate over $3M quickly.
- **Scale check:** on a $10M estate, a death after 7/1/2026 vs just before saves roughly $200,000 from the rate rollback — illustrative; compute against the current DOR rate table.

## 3. Community property
- Washington is a community property state. Both halves of community property receive a **full basis step-up at the first spouse's death** (IRC §1014(b)(6)) — the "double step-up," a major income-tax advantage over common-law states and a reason to be careful before retitling appreciated community assets as separate property.
- Community property agreements (CPAs) can confirm character and secure step-up treatment, but they interact with trust-based estate plans — coordinate the CPA and any credit-shelter design with the estate attorney rather than treating them independently.
- Characterization matters for the business: an LLC interest acquired during marriage is presumptively community property — relevant to estate math and to any future sale.

## 4. WA Cares Fund (LTSS Trust)
- **Tax:** 0.58% of gross W-2 wages, employee-paid, no wage cap. **Self-employment income is NOT subject** unless the person opts in.
- **Benefit:** up to $36,500 lifetime (inflation-adjusted), claimable from July 1, 2026.
- **SB 5291 (2025; most provisions 1/1/2026):** out-of-state portability for vested contributors and expanded vesting pathways (10-year, or 3-of-last-6-years).
- The original private-LTC-insurance opt-out window is closed (it required coverage in place before 11/1/2021).
- **Planning note:** $36,500 is a partial offset, not long-term-care coverage. Real LTC exposure still warrants the traditional-vs-hybrid analysis in `frameworks.md`; product purchase remains a hard route.
